# Vision

**CraftApps — Craft your own apps.**

## The problem

I rely on many apps and services for small, personal tasks, and so do most people. Many of them want a subscription, an account, or my data. Meanwhile, AI agents can now write working software for people who have never programmed. The hard question is no longer *how* to build, but *what*.

Most people, asked "what app would you build for yourself?", draw a blank. Their needs are hidden in habits: the same request to an AI every week, the spreadsheet updated by hand every Monday, the idea written down three times and never pursued.

## The idea

CraftApps finds those needs in **evidence** the person chooses to share — conversations, notes, routines, projects — instead of generic idea lists. Then it guides an AI agent to turn the one they pick into working software they own and understand.

## What gets made

**Native apps for the devices the person already uses** — iOS and macOS for an iPhone and Mac user, Android and Windows for an Android and Windows user. Native apps keep data on the device, work offline, and need no server or account, which is what ownership and privacy require.

**Few dependencies.** Platforms already include what most personal apps need. A crafted app uses them, and adds a library, server, account, or cross-platform layer only for a clear reason: less to break, update, and trust, and an app that keeps working for years.

When an app would be overkill, the answer is smaller: a Shortcut, a script, a scheduled task, or a feature of an app the person already has. Websites and hosted services are not the goal; they need servers, accounts, and someone else's infrastructure.

## Principles

- **Security and privacy first — above everything else.** A technical requirement, not a slogan. Private conversations are never handed to an AI raw: they are cleaned on the person's own computer, reviewed by them, and only then read. Nothing is sent to anyone else. When security conflicts with convenience, security wins.
- **Own your software.** Users control their applications, source code, and data.
- **Understand what you own.** Ownership is not only having the source code; it is knowing how your software works, where its data lives, and how to change it. CraftApps teaches people to become creators, not just recipients of generated code.
- **Small is beautiful.** Not every problem needs a large application, a backend, or a subscription. Sometimes the right answer is a shortcut, a script, or a better prompt.
- **Build for yourself first.** Software is valuable even if only one person uses it.
- **Accessible to everyone.** No programming experience required.
- **Open and extensible.** Skills are plain text: readable, reusable, customizable.
- **Human agency.** AI assists; people decide. Consequential actions need explicit approval.

## What I am not doing

- Replacing every existing application. If a good tool already solves the problem, CraftApps says so.
- Building a hosted platform or a paid marketplace.
- Collecting data. The skills run inside the person's own agent, on their own machine.

## The long-term picture

Someone says:

> "Find something useful I could build for myself."

`discover-app` finds an opportunity grounded in their real behavior, and they choose it. `build-app` checks whether an app already does it, plans a small native app with them, builds it, verifies it, and explains how it works. If they want, `share-app` shares it — with one person or with everyone.

The result belongs to them. No unnecessary subscription. No mandatory account. No personal data handed over. Just software built around a person's actual needs.

And if a thousand people need the same small app, it shouldn't be built a thousand times. That is the idea behind the **CraftApps Library**, which is planned: a free, public list of apps people made with CraftApps and chose to share. `build-app` will look there first, so a good personal app can become a people's project that others use, adapt, and improve instead of rebuilding it. Every app is reviewed before it is listed, and sharing is always optional. Submissions by email are already welcome.
